## Observations
OBSERVATION: ERC-4361 is a Final Standards Track ERC for off-chain authentication where Ethereum accounts sign a standard message format to establish sessions, with goals of providing a self-custodied alternative to centralized identity providers and improving interoperability across off-chain services (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
OBSERVATION: ERC-4361's motivation states that signing in to popular non-blockchain services today typically uses centralized identity providers such as large internet companies and email providers, and that SIWE offers a self-custodial option for users who wish to assume more control and responsibility over their own digital identity (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
OBSERVATION: The SIWE workflow requires the relying party to generate a SIWE message, the wallet to present it for signing with ERC-191 signed data format, and the relying party to check signature validity and SIWE message content (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
OBSERVATION: SIWE messages include domain, address, URI, version, chain ID, nonce, issued-at, and optional expiration time, not-before, request ID, and resources fields (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).

## Reasoning
INFERENCE: Because ERC-4361 explicitly positions SIWE as a self-custodied alternative to centralized identity providers and places responsibility on users for their own digital identity, SIWE-only login would create friction for non-crypto users who lack wallets, seed-phrase management experience, or ETH for gas (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
INFERENCE: A consumer NFT ticketing app expecting mostly non-crypto users should offer email/passkey login with an embedded wallet as the primary path to maximize onboarding conversion, while also supporting SIWE so crypto-native users can connect self-custodied wallets (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
INFERENCE: The top three trade-offs are: (1) onboarding conversion — email/passkey with embedded wallet removes wallet creation and gas barriers for non-crypto buyers, while SIWE-only would exclude them; (2) custody/security — embedded wallets shift key custody to the provider or a custodial model, introducing recovery convenience but centralization risk, whereas SIWE keeps keys self-custodied with user responsibility; (3) compliance — email/passkey enables familiar KYC/AML and custodial obligations, while SIWE-only complicates identity linkage and regulatory attribution (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).

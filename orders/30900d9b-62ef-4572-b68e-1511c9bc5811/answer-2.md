ANSWER: Offer email/passkey login with an embedded wallet alongside Sign-In with Ethereum (EIP-4361); do not use SIWE only.

## Observations
OBSERVATION: ERC-4361 is a Final Standards Track specification for off-chain authentication where Ethereum accounts sign a standard message format parameterized by scope, session details, and security mechanisms such as a nonce, to establish sessions (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
OBSERVATION: ERC-4361's goals are to provide a self-custodied alternative to centralized identity providers, improve interoperability across off-chain services for Ethereum-based authentication, and give wallet vendors a consistent machine-readable message format for improved user experiences and consent management (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
OBSERVATION: ERC-4361's motivation states that users of popular non-blockchain services typically use centralized identity providers such as large internet companies and email providers, and that incentives are often misaligned between these parties (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
OBSERVATION: ERC-4361 offers a self-custodial option for users who wish to assume more control and responsibility over their own digital identity (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
OBSERVATION: In the SIWE flow, the relying party generates a SIWE message, the wallet presents it for signing with ERC-191, and the relying party verifies the signature and message content before establishing a session (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
OBSERVATION: No source excerpts about passkeys, embedded wallets, email login, onboarding conversion rates, custody models, or compliance obligations were successfully fetched for this step (source: service source reader output; only source 1 was read).

## Reasoning
INFERENCE: Because ERC-4361 requires users to hold and sign with an Ethereum account, a SIWE-only login forces non-crypto users to create a wallet, manage keys, and sign messages before purchasing an NFT ticket, which adds steps that centralized email/passkey login avoids (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
INFERENCE: For a consumer NFT ticketing app expecting mostly non-crypto users, offering email/passkey login with an embedded wallet lowers onboarding friction by letting users authenticate with familiar credentials while the embedded wallet handles blockchain interactions behind the scenes (source: reasoning from https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
INFERENCE: Keeping SIWE as an option preserves self-custody and interoperability for crypto-native users who want control over their identity, matching ERC-4361's stated goal (source: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361).
INFERENCE: The top three trade-offs are: (1) onboarding conversion — email/passkey with embedded wallet should convert better for non-crypto users by removing wallet setup and gas friction, while SIWE-only may deter users unfamiliar with wallets; (2) custody/security — embedded wallets shift custody responsibility to the app or a provider, introducing platform risk and key-recovery challenges, whereas SIWE keeps keys self-custodied but places security burden on users; (3) compliance — email/passkey flows likely trigger KYC, AML, and data-protection obligations tied to identifiable users, while SIWE's pseudonymous addresses may reduce some identity collection but do not eliminate ticketing or financial-regulation duties (source: reasoning from https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4361; no public compliance or custody sources were fetched).
